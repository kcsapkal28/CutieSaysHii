## The visual bar

Decks are judged against hand-built explainer decks of the kind a top studio would make for a keynote or a great lecture. They cover any subject: engineering, science, history, business, art, education. The subject changes; the craft does not. What that craft looks like:

- **One idea per slide**, stated as a sentence-case claim ("Five one-way channels, each with its own handshake", "Most of the ocean's heat sits below 700 metres"), and a visual that *is* the explanation. No bullet lists, no paragraphs on the slide, no clip-art icons, no decorative stock imagery, no card grids of text.
- **The visual is a working model of the idea**: a queue that actually overflows when you slow the consumer, packets that actually travel their channels, a supply curve that actually shifts the price when you drag demand, a river delta that actually builds outward step by step, a timeline that actually compresses to show acceleration. The viewer learns by watching it happen or by poking it.
- **Procedural 3D, when the subject has form**: recognisable objects built from primitives (lathe, capsule, extrude, rounded boxes), physically based materials, a key and a rim light, contact shadows, filling 40–60% of the canvas, exploded or cut away to show structure, with numbered callout chips on leader lines that end exactly on their parts. Never a tiny dark object lost in the void, never 3D for its own sake.
- **Typography as image when the words are the point**: one enormous number, a single quotation set beautifully, a word that changes meaning across steps.
- **Labels that land**: every callout connects to its thing; colour that encodes meaning has a legend; numbers have units.
- **Restraint**: two typefaces, a near-neutral base with one to three accents that each *mean* something and keep that meaning across the deck, large type (titles 64–96px, captions 30–40px, labels 22–28px on a 1920×1080 canvas), and lots of air.
- **Motion with purpose**: flow shows direction, steps reveal causality one beat at a time, transitions carry the eye from cause to effect. Nothing moves just to decorate.
- **A designed whole**: a recurring motif (a hero object, a colour code, a label style) ties the slides together, while the compositions vary from slide to slide.
