## The visual bar

Decks are judged against hand-built explainer decks a top studio would make for a keynote or a great lecture, on any subject:

- **One idea per slide**, a sentence-case claim, and a visual that *is* the explanation: a working model the viewer watches happen or pokes (a queue that overflows, a curve that shifts, a boundary that grows step by step). No bullet lists, paragraphs, clip-art, stock imagery or card grids of text.
- **The form that fits**: 3D when form matters, a simulation when rules matter, a moving diagram for a process, a chart for numbers, type as image when the number is the point.
- **A designed whole**: two typefaces, a near-neutral base, one to three accents that each mean one thing across the deck, a recurring motif, varied compositions. Motion only to show cause, direction or change.

First drafts fail on the same few things, each checkable on the 1920×1080 screenshot. Reviews file every issue under one of these kinds:

1. `labels`: each callout names one thing and touches it: a 1.5–2px leader from the chip's nearest edge ends in a 6–8px dot *on* the part. No chip sits on another chip, a leader, the title or the focal element. Colour that encodes meaning has a legend (or direct labels) from the step it appears. Numbers carry units.
2. `steps`: each step changes the picture at a glance from the back of the room: something appears, moves, grows, splits or recolours over a large part of the visual, or the key number changes. A moved highlight, a faded chip or a new caption alone is not a step. Each step is a complete composition; the last shows the whole picture.
3. `overlap`: nothing covers anything unless deliberately layered; text never crosses text, lines or marks; ≥ 24px between neighbouring blocks.
4. `sparse`: the main visual covers ≥ 50% of the canvas and fills its region; no dead quadrant; marks ≥ 8px; a hero object 40–60% of the canvas. Air sits between elements, not in one empty corner.
5. `contrast`: text ≥ 4.5:1 against what is actually behind it (≥ 3:1 from 24px); meaningful lines and shapes ≥ 3:1; dimmed past states still ≥ 3:1.
6. `storyboard`: everything the storyboard names (objects, callouts, numbers, colours, controls) is visible by the last step, from its beat's step, in its named colour.
7. `tiny-text`, `clipped`: title 64–88px, captions 30–38px, labels 22–28px, nothing under 18px; text inside x 96…1824, y 72…1008, never cut by the canvas or its box.
8. `accuracy`, `form`: the drawing obeys the model and the data (curves from the formula, values to scale, the lit side facing the light); objects read as what they are, lit by a key and a rim light, framed whole.
